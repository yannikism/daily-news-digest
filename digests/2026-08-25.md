## Extreme Weather

### Rare "particularly dangerous situation" fire-weather warning hits the Pacific Northwest

The Storm Prediction Center posted its highest fire-weather category — "extremely critical" — over the Columbia Basin and parts of central and eastern Washington into north-central Oregon this week, with relative humidity forecast to fall to 15–20% and sustained winds of 20–30 mph gusting past 40–45 mph. NWS offices in Spokane and Pendleton issued Particularly Dangerous Situation (PDS) Red Flag Warnings — a rare designation reserved for conditions that greatly exceed standard red-flag criteria — covering the Waterville Plateau, Okanogan Valley, Spokane area, Colville Reservation, and the Palouse and Snake River areas. Nationally, more than 7.6 million acres have burned across over 50,000 fires so far in 2026, running near 185% of the 10-year average.

### Phoenix heat warning extended through August 29 as heat deaths outpace 2025

The National Weather Service's Phoenix office extended its Extreme Heat Warning, issued August 24, through 8 p.m. MST on August 29, with afternoon temperatures forecast between 106°F and 118°F. Phoenix hit 115°F over the weekend, breaking the prior record of 114°F set in 2025, driven by a persistent strong high-pressure system. Maricopa County's medical examiner has confirmed 81 heat-related deaths so far in 2026, more than double the 35 confirmed at the same point last year.
