### Nepalese youth climate group and 350.org call Himalayan flood a "clear message" on climate delay

Following the Nepal-Tibet glacier-collapse flood, Aakriti Dotel, national network coordinator of Nepalese Youth for Climate Action, said August 27 that "the devastating floods in Rasuwa are another painful reminder of the growing climate crisis facing Nepal and the Himalayas." 350.org said in a statement the same day that "every destabilized glacier in a warming Himalaya is a ticking time bomb that can go off unannounced, and when it does, those least responsible for the climate crisis pay with their lives," calling on wealthy nations to deliver climate finance to frontline communities.

## Extreme Weather

### Nepal-Tibet glacier collapse flood: death toll passes 470, nearly 1,000 still missing

A section of a Himalayan glacier broke away at about 5,200 meters elevation on Wednesday, August 26, and plunged roughly 1,200 meters onto the valley floor, triggering a flash flood down the Lhende River that tore through villages along the Nepal-Tibet border. Nepal's prime minister's office reported 469 confirmed deaths and 977 people still missing as of Friday, while Chinese authorities reported at least 5 dead and 558 missing in Tibet. More than 500 foreigners from 32 countries are unaccounted for, including roughly 90 Americans.

[Source](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/8/28/nepal-and-china-halt-rescue-efforts-as-border-barrier-lake-overflows) · Al Jazeera

### Barrier lake formed by the flood starts overflowing, halting rescue operations

A "barrier lake" formed when Wednesday's flood dumped huge volumes of mud and debris into a steep valley began overflowing Friday, August 28, according to officials in Nepal's Rasuwa district. Chinese rescue teams pulled back from the Gyirong county lake as the risk of a breach rose, and Nepalese authorities paused search operations for 90 minutes. The lake holds an estimated 2 million cubic meters of water, with another 3 million cubic meters expected to flow in over the following three days, raising the risk of a second flood wave downstream.

### Tropical Storm Dolly dissipates into an open tropical wave

Tropical Storm Dolly, the Atlantic season's fourth named storm, formed Thursday, August 27, but by late Friday morning had weakened back into an open tropical wave, choked off by surrounding Saharan dust and dry air and unfavorable upper-level winds. Its remnants, moving west near 25 mph, are forecast to bring heavy rain and possible flash flooding to the Leeward Islands beginning Saturday night. It has been the quietest start to an Atlantic hurricane season in nearly 40 years, with three short-lived tropical storms and no hurricanes so far, which forecasters attribute largely to a strengthening El Niño.
